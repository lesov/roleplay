# CODEX OF THE INNER SEA — THE WORLD AT 1496 DR

> **A traveler's account of the lands about the Sea of Fallen Stars, their crowns, councils, and quarrels, as they stand in the year 1496 of Dalereckoning.**
>
> *(Body text is in-world / player-facing presentation layer. The* **INTERNAL APPENDIX** *at the end lists every deviation from established lore and the mapping hook behind it — strip it from any player build.)*

---

## THE STATE OF THE WORLD

A decade has passed since the Sundering remade the heavens. The gods walk nearer to the world again, drowned lands have risen, and powers thought dead have returned wearing old crowns. The great cataclysms are spent; what remains is the slower, crueler work of ambition.

The age's defining death is the fall of **Netheril**. For two generations the shade-lords of that resurrected empire cast their shadow over the north and east, buying kings and seeding ruin. In 1487 DR their flying city burned from the sky and their empire shattered overnight. No single power has risen to fill the void. Instead a dozen crowns and councils circle the wreckage, each certain the next age belongs to it.

The center of that circling is the **Sea of Fallen Stars** — the Inner Sea, the wine-dark heart of Faerûn, ringed by the richest cities and oldest grudges in the world. On its northern shore stand the ambitious crown of **Cormyr** and the gold-fat counting-houses of **Sembia**, with the small free **Dales** and the haunted elven wood of **Cormanthor** between them. To the south sprawl the quarrelling jewel-cities of the **Vilhon Reach** — fractious **Chessenta**, mercantile **Chondath** and **Turmish** — too rich to defend themselves and too proud to unite. In the far southeast the god-kings of **Mulhorand** and the newly-returned **Unther** rule as they did a thousand years ago.

And in the east squats **Thay**. The realm of the Red Wizards, governed by an immortal who is no longer truly alive, grows hungrier every year. Only embattled **Aglarond** stands between the magocracy and the soft underbelly of the Inner Sea, and Aglarond stands increasingly alone.

Far to the west, beyond the Sword Coast cities and the prestige of **Waterdeep**, the merchant-princes of **Amn** grow obscenely rich on silver hauled across the Trackless Sea from the conquered shores of **Maztica** — the True World, a continent of feathered serpents and blood-altars that most folk of the Heartlands believe a sailor's lie. That silver is beginning to buy armies on this side of the ocean. Few yet understand what that will mean.

The world is at an uneasy, well-armed peace. It will not last the decade.

## CORMYR — THE FOREST KINGDOM

Capital **Suzail** · Ruled by **House Obarskyr** · The oldest continuous crown in Faerûn

Cormyr is the model of what a kingdom should be: fertile, disciplined, fiercely loyal, and very old. The Obarskyr line has held the Forest Kingdom for fourteen centuries, defended by the iron Purple Dragon legions and the dread **War Wizards**, whose battle-magic no rival can match in open field. Its courts are brilliant, its roads are safe, and its self-regard is boundless.

For a generation Cormyr looked inward, rebuilding after the goblin wars and the loss of King Azoun the Fourth. That season is ending. A new generation of Obarskyrs has come of age in the saddle and the salle — young, martial, and impatient with their elders' caution. In the council chambers of Suzail the talk is no longer of defense but of *rights*: ancient Obarskyr claims, by old marriage and older treaty, to crowns south of the Sea of Fallen Stars. The heralds have begun, quietly, to dust off the genealogies.

Cormyr does not think of itself as an aggressor. It thinks of itself as the rightful order of the world, temporarily mislaid. When its banners cross the Inner Sea — and they will — it will be to *restore* what is Cormyr's, and woe to those who call it conquest.

## SEMBIA — THE GOLDEN CONFEDERATION

Capital **Selgaunt** · Ruled by an oligarchy of merchant-houses · The richest land per acre in Faerûn

Sembia has no king and wants none. It is ruled by money, and money has many heads — a shifting council of the great trading families of Selgaunt, Yhaunn, and Saerb, who buy fleets, hire armies, and elect figureheads as the markets require. For decades Sembia wore the collar of Netheril, its wealth bled to feed the shade-lords. With Netheril dead, that collar is off, and Sembia has remembered how to be greedy on its own account.

No nation on the Inner Sea floats more cargo or coins more profit. Sembian carracks crowd every harbor from the Dragon Reach to the Vilhon; Sembian banking-houses hold the paper of half the crowns of Faerûn. This is precisely why Sembia is hated. Its neighbors look at all that ungoverned, undefended wealth — a republic of shopkeepers with no warrior-caste and no friends — and they do not see a partner. They see a prize that has not yet been divided.

Sembia knows it. It survives by playing every court against the others, switching sides without shame and buying its way out of every war it cannot bribe its way around. It is rarely loved and never quite destroyed.

## THE DALELANDS — THE FREE FOLK

A confederation of small dales · No central crown · Bound by the Dales Compact

Between Cormyr's borders and Cormanthor's eaves lie the Dales: a scatter of small, stubborn, self-governing folk — Shadowdale, Archendale, Battledale, Mistledale, and the rest — who answer to no king and barely to one another. They meet in the Dales Council, argue, and disperse. They are farmers and foresters first, but the Dales breed something the great powers cannot: free men who fight for no lord's blood-right and will not break.

Out of that tradition has grown the Dales' chief export besides grain. The **free companies** of the Dalelands — disciplined pike-and-blade mercenaries who sell their service to the highest honest bidder — are reckoned the finest heavy infantry on the Inner Sea, and the courts of Cormyr, Sembia, and the Vilhon all bid for their banners come campaign season. A Dalesman captain's word, once given, is kept; this is worth more than any oath of nobility, and everyone knows it.

The Dales want only to be left alone. The age will not grant the wish. Whoever masters the northern shore must either buy the Dales or break them, and breaking them has been tried before.

---

## CORMANTHOR — THE FALLEN WOOD

The great forest · Contested · No unified rule

The vast forest of Cormanthor was once the seat of an elven glory; twice the elves have tried to raise **Myth Drannor** from its ruins, and twice the City of Song has burned. Its last fall, in the wars that ended Netheril, scattered the elves anew. Today the wood is a borderland without a border-lord: withdrawn elven enclaves, drow raiders welling up from below, and Sembian loggers and Cormyrean foresters nibbling the edges. It is a place of ruins, ambushes, and old magic, and it sits squarely between the two great northern powers like a held breath.

---

## CHESSENTA — THE QUARRELLING CROWN

A league of rival city-states · Cimbar, Soorenar, Airspur, Luthcheq, and a dozen more · No master since the god-king fell

Chessenta is the most brilliant and most divided land on the Inner Sea. Its cities are jewels of art, philosophy, and the arena — and they loathe one another with a passion only neighbors can sustain. For a few violent years a returned dragon god-king bound them by force; with his fall, the leash snapped, and Chessenta sprang back into its natural state of glittering, murderous rivalry. Cimbar plots against Soorenar, Soorenar against Airspur, and all of them hire the same mercenaries to do it.

**Cimbar**, the eldest and wealthiest, is the prize the others circle — and, increasingly, the prize that powers beyond Chessenta circle too. A land this rich, this skilled, and this incapable of uniting is an invitation. The Chessentans, supremely confident in their own cleverness, are certain they can hire and out-maneuver any foreign army that dares the Reach. They have never been more wrong, and they will learn it expensively.

In the south, the crown of **Soorenar** styles itself a true kingdom rather than a mere city, and its disputed succession is exactly the sort of legal tangle an ambitious foreign monarch could call a *claim*.

---

## CHONDATH AND THE VIGILANT CHURCH

Capital **Arrabar** · A mercantile state and the seat of the Tormtar · Wields a sword in one hand and a writ in the other

Chondath was once the empire of the Vilhon, and though its temporal power has long faded, Arrabar remains the Reach's spiritual capital. For the great **Church of Torm the Vigilant** — the most organized, wealthiest, and most politically formidable faith of the Inner Sea — keeps its high seat there. The Church holds lands, levies tithes from a dozen realms, fields its own armies of templars, and crowns and excommunicates kings. Its blessing legitimizes a throne; its censure can unmake one.

This makes Arrabar the pivot of Inner Sea politics. Every great power courts the Church and fears it, and the Church, jealously guarding its independence between far stronger neighbors, plays them against each other with the cold skill of an institution that means to outlive them all. It will make alliances and betray them, bless wars and curse them, all to keep any single crown from growing strong enough to dominate the faith. A churchman of Arrabar would tell you this is the work of preserving the divine order. A cynic would call it statecraft in a mitre. Both are correct.

## TURMISH — THE MERCHANT SHORE

Capital **Alaghôn** · A trading republic of the southern Inner Sea

Turmish is Chondath's quieter cousin: a prosperous merchant-state on the southern shore, governed by guild-assemblies, devoted to commerce and to the green faiths of Silvanus and Eldath whose druids keep the Reach's forests. It builds good ships and asks few questions about cargo. In the coming storms Turmish will want above all to stay neutral and stay rich; the storms will not cooperate.

---

## THAY — THE REALM OF THE RED WIZARDS

Capital **Eltabbar** · Ruled by **Szass Tam** and the Council of Zulkirs · A nation built upon slavery and the dead

There is no power in the east, perhaps in all Faerûn, more feared than Thay. It is governed in name by eight zulkirs, archmage-tyrants each supreme in one school of magic. It is governed in truth by one of them: **Szass Tam**, the lich-regent, master of necromancy, who won Thay's long civil war and has been reshaping the realm ever since into something monstrous — a state run by the undead, for the undead, in which the living are merely the most renewable of resources.

Thay's strength is threefold. Its Red Wizards are the deadliest battle-mages in the world after Cormyr's, and far more numerous. Its slave-driven economy and **trade enclaves**, planted like ticks in cities across Faerûn, fund armies without limit. And its corsair fleets, raised from the Pirate Isles and crewed by oarsmen who do not tire because they no longer breathe, are beginning to contest the open waters of the Inner Sea.

Szass Tam is patient, as only the deathless can be. He does not need to win in a year. He intends to swallow the Inner Sea one frontier, one fleet, one broken kingdom at a time — and the western powers, absorbed in quarrelling over the Vilhon, are obligingly looking the other way.

---

## AGLAROND — THE LAST BULWARK

Capital **Velprintalar** · A magocracy of the eastern peninsula · Thay's oldest enemy, and the West's forgotten shield

For three centuries Aglarond has done one thing supremely well: hold the line against Thay. The forested peninsula and its half-elven folk are guarded by a tradition of war-mages and by the dread reputation of its witch-rulers, and time and again Aglarond has thrown the Red Wizards back from its passes. It is the cork in the bottle. So long as Aglarond stands, Thay cannot pour west into Thesk, the Dales, and the rich northern shore.

But Aglarond is small, it is alone, and it is tired. The western crowns send fine words and no soldiers; to Cormyr and Sembia, Thay is a distant rumor and Aglarond a faraway name. The kingdom's rulers know the cold arithmetic better than anyone: every year Thay grows and Aglarond does not, and one year the line will break. They mean to make that year cost the Red Wizards dearly. They do not expect to be rescued.

---

## THESK AND THE WIZARDS' REACH — THE MARCHES BETWEEN

A belt of contested city-states east of Aglarond · The Golden Way to the lands of the rising sun

East of Aglarond lies the bruised borderland: **Thesk**, gateway to the long eastern trade-road and home to merchants from the distant Shou lands, and the scattered free cities of the **Wizards' Reach** along the southern shore. These towns have been fought over, sacked, and traded between Thay and Aglarond so many times that loyalty here is a season's lease. Whoever wins the east will win it here first, town by town.

---

## MULHORAND — THE LAND OF THE GOD-KINGS

Capital **Skuld** · A theocracy of returned divine incarnations · Ancient, golden, and insular

In the far southeast, the oldest order in the world has reasserted itself. The gods of Mulhorand walk again in mortal flesh, their incarnations seated on the thrones their faith held a hundred generations ago, and under them the realm has thrown off foreign yokes and turned inward to its temples, its great river, and its enormous, ancient wealth. Mulhorand is proud, slow, and rich beyond the dreams of the Vilhon — which is to say it is exactly the sort of glittering, complacent prize an expanding eastern empire dreams of. Thay's mapmakers have already drawn the borders they intend.

---

## UNTHER — THE RETURNED EMPIRE

Capital **Unthalass** · The god-king **Gilgeam** restored · Newly risen, newly aggressive

For an age Unther was simply gone, its land swallowed and its people scattered. With the Sundering it came roaring back, dragging its territory and its tyrant-god out of exile together. **Gilgeam** the God-King rules again, and Unther has returned in a fury — reclaiming its ancient soil, glaring at Mulhorand across old grievances, and warring with the dragon-folk who settled the empty land in its absence. It is unstable, vengeful, and looking for someone to conquer in order to feel whole again.

---

## AMN — THE COUNCIL OF SILVER

Capital **Athkatla** · Ruled by the secretive **Council of Six** · Master of the Maztican trade

West of the Inner Sea, beyond the mountains, the merchant-state of Amn worships profit as openly as other lands worship gods. Its true rulers are six masked oligarchs whose identities are a state secret, and their single obsession is gain. Amn's masterstroke was the sea: generations ago its expeditions crossed the Trackless Ocean to **Maztica**, the True World, and Amn has been hauling its plundered silver home ever since.

That silver has made Amn the banker of the western world — and now, increasingly, of the Inner Sea's wars. Amnian coin flows east into the counting-houses of Sembia and the war-chests of any coalition that can pay the interest. The Council of Six cares nothing for crowns, claims, or churches; it cares which side of a war it has funded, and whether the debt will be honored. Where the silver fleets go, Amnian influence follows, and the silver fleets now sail to the heart of Faerûn.

---

## WATERDEEP AND THE LORDS' ALLIANCE — THE PRESTIGE OF THE NORTH

The City of Splendors · Ruled by the Open Lord and the Masked Lords · First among the Sword Coast powers

Far to the northwest, Waterdeep is the grandest free city in the world — cosmopolitan, wealthy, and ruled by an Open Lord and a council of hidden Masked Lords. Around it Waterdeep has gathered the **Lords' Alliance**, a league of northern cities and rulers sworn to mutual defense, and through it Waterdeep speaks with a voice that carries far beyond its walls.

The City of Splendors is too distant to covet the Vilhon and too proud to ignore the Inner Sea entirely. It intervenes in eastern affairs the way a great power intervenes in another continent's quarrels: selectively, self-interestedly, and always to keep any single rival from growing too strong. An Alliance fleet or an Alliance purse can tip a war on the far side of Faerûn — and then sail home before the reckoning. Waterdeep makes alliances the way other cities make trade deals, and breaks them just as cleanly.

---

## THE PIRATE ISLES — THE CORSAIR SEA

The broken archipelago at the heart of the Inner Sea · No law but the strong

In the middle of the Sea of Fallen Stars lie the Pirate Isles, a maze of rocks and hidden anchorages that has never known a master. For centuries its corsairs have preyed on Inner Sea shipping and laughed at the navies sent to burn them out. Lately the laughter has a colder edge: the most powerful of the corsair-lords have taken Thayan gold, Thayan magic, and Thayan dead men for their oars, and the Pirate Isles are becoming less a nest of free brigands than the forward fleet of an eastern empire. The merchant captains of Sembia and Turmish have begun to notice that the pirates raid where Thay would profit, and spare where Thay would not.

---

## MAZTICA — THE TRUE WORLD

A continent across the Trackless Sea · The Aztec lands of the feathered serpent and the blood-altar · Amn's conquest and treasure

Most folk of the Heartlands think Maztica a tavern fable. It is not. Across the western ocean lies a whole continent of jungle empires, step-pyramids, and gods who demand hearts — the realm of **Qotal** the Plumed Serpent and **Zaltec** the war-god — where Amnian conquerors carved out colonies a hundred years ago and have been extracting silver, gold, and slaves ever since. The True World is a second theater of the age, running to its own brutal rhythm of conquest, rebellion, and plague, and the bullion it bleeds is the hidden engine of every war on the Inner Sea. Whoever controls the silver fleets controls the means to fight; and the silver fleets, fat and slow on the long crossing home, are the richest prize afloat.

# INTERNAL APPENDIX — CANON DEVIATIONS & MAPPING HOOKS
> **STRIP FROM ALL PLAYER BUILDS.** This records where the codex departs from established Forgotten Realms lore and the historical-scaffold reason behind each change. Body text above is clean presentation layer; this is scaffold metadata.

**General period note.** Baseline is the post–Second Sundering 5e era at 1496 DR: Netheril/Thultanthar fell 1487 DR, Luruar dissolved after the orc war of 1484–85, the gods and several lost realms returned. All canon-accurate. The "well-armed peace about to break" framing is original, to seat the campaign at the *Charles VIII invades Italy (1494)* starting line.

- **Cormyr → Valois France (aggressor monarchy).** Canon Cormyr under King Foril Obarskyr I is stable and inward-looking. *Deviation:* recast as ambitious and expansionist, with a rising martial generation and dusted-off dynastic claims south of the Inner Sea, to drive the Charles VIII → Louis XII → Francis I sequence of conquering kings. Specific rulers left unnamed so you can install the three-monarch succession from the timeline.
- **Sembia → Venice (rich, resented republic).** Canon-accurate as a wealthy merchant oligarchy freed from Netherese control in 1487. *Deviation (emphasis only):* foregrounded as the universally-envied, undefended naval-trade power everyone plans to partition — sets up the League of Cambrai (First Great War, 1510 DR).
- **Dalelands → the Swiss / small free states.** *Deviation:* invented a prestigious heavy-infantry **free-company** tradition. Canon dalesfolk are farmers/rangers, not renowned pike mercenaries. Needed so the "Swiss pikemen" can dominate then be broken (Marignano 1517 DR / Bicocca 1524 DR).
- **Cormanthor.** Canon-accurate (ruined Myth Drannor, scattered elves, contested wood). No mapping load; included as the central northern borderland.
- **Chessenta → the Italian city-states; Cimbar = Milan; Soorenar = Naples.** Canon-accurate as fractious Greek-style city-states recently freed from the god-king Tchazzar. *Deviation:* Cimbar elevated to "the prize duchy" and Soorenar styled a kingdom with a disputed succession, to host the contested-throne casus belli. *[city names swappable]*
- **Chondath / Arrabar → the Papal States; Church of Torm = the Papacy.** *Major deviation.* Canon Arrabar/Chondath is a secular, declining merchant state; there is no dominant territorial church there. We have installed a powerful, land-holding, king-making **Church of Torm the Vigilant** seated at Arrabar to serve as the Papacy analog (holds territory, flips sides, suffers the Schism in 1519 DR, is sacked in 1529 DR). *[deity & seat swappable — keep consistent with timeline/spec]*
- **Turmish.** Canon-accurate. Minor battleground flavor only.
- **Thay → the Ottoman Empire.** Canon-accurate (Szass Tam's undead magocracy, Red Wizards, enclaves, slavery). *Deviation (emphasis):* amplified westward/maritime expansionism and corsair-fleet ties to support the Eastern Tide schedule and the naval-supremacy beat (Preveza 1540 DR).
- **Aglarond → Hungary (the shield that breaks).** Canon-accurate as Thay's embattled western rival. *Deviation:* foregrounded its isolation and the West's neglect, to make its scheduled destruction in 1528 DR (Mohács) tragic and earned. Ruler left unnamed (the Simbul's post-Sundering status is canonically murky); install your own monarch who can die at the breaking.
- **Thesk / Wizards' Reach → the Balkan-Anatolian marches.** Canon-accurate contested borderland. No deviation.
- **Mulhorand → Mamluk Egypt.** Canon-accurate resurgent god-king theocracy. *Deviation (emphasis):* framed as the rich, complacent prize Thay covets, to seat the "Thay swallows part of the ancient south" beat (Ottoman conquest of Egypt, 1517).
- **Unther → the contested Levant / a returned aggressor.** Canon-accurate (Gilgeam and Unther returned in the Sundering, at war with Tymanther). No real deviation; positioned as a volatile southeastern wildcard. (Tymanther/the dragonborn omitted from the codex body for focus — add if you want the Unther–Tymanther war as a side theater.)
- **Amn → Castile / the New World silver engine.** Canon-accurate (Council of Six; Amn conquered Maztica in 1361 DR). *Deviation:* extended Amn's relevance eastward as the Grand Alliance's banker and silver-supplier, since canon Amn is Sword-Coast-focused. This is the load-bearing "Spanish silver funds the wars" link.
- **Waterdeep / Lords' Alliance → England (peripheral opportunist).** Canon-accurate institutions. *Deviation:* extended their diplomatic/naval reach into Inner Sea affairs, which canon keeps mostly confined to the Sword Coast and North.
- **Pirate Isles → the Barbary corsairs.** Canon-accurate pirate haven. *Deviation:* bound them to Thay (the Barbarossa/Ottoman-admiral parallel) to enable Thayan naval supremacy.
- **Maztica → the New World.** Canon-accurate (Aztec analog, Amnian conquest, Qotal/Zaltec). No deviation; included as the second theater and silver source per the spec's §8.

**Not yet placed (optional levers):** the Moonsea cities / the **Zhentarim** make a natural extra member or wildcard for the Grand Alliance bloc if you want a fifth great-power voice in the west. **Netheril's fall (1487 DR)** is available as a "recently-collapsed hegemon" backstory if you ever want a power-vacuum event to predate the campaign's wars.
